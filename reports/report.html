Gauntlet

Adversarial co-evolution arena for prompt-injection defenses · 3 seeds · 5 rounds × 12 generations · population 200

What the numbers say

  • naive: mean recall on a never-seen attack family is 86.6% (in-distribution 100.0%); weakest family is indirect at 30%.
  • hardened: mean recall on a never-seen attack family is 96.7% (in-distribution 100.0%); weakest family is indirect at 74%.
  • windowed: mean recall on a never-seen attack family is 96.5% (in-distribution 99.9%); weakest family is indirect at 89%.
  • transformer: mean recall on a never-seen attack family is 87.6% (in-distribution 100.0%); weakest family is indirect at 13%.
  • transformer windowed: mean recall on a never-seen attack family is 96.9% (in-distribution 99.7%); weakest family is indirect at 82%.
  • naive under attack: evolved attackers evade it 75% of the time in round 1 and 1% in round 5 (peak later round: 6%).
  • hardened under attack: evolved attackers evade it 91% of the time in round 1 and 0% in round 5 (peak later round: 25%).
  • windowed under attack: evolved attackers evade it 46% of the time in round 1 and 27% in round 5 (peak later round: 36%).
  • transformer under attack: evolved attackers evade it 75% of the time in round 1 and 0% in round 5 (peak later round: 10%).
  • transformer windowed under attack: evolved attackers evade it 55% of the time in round 1 and 0% in round 5 (peak later round: 15%).
  • naive on real human-written injections: AUC 0.57 before adversarial training, 0.60 after.
  • hardened on real human-written injections: AUC 0.67 before adversarial training, 0.71 after.
  • windowed on real human-written injections: AUC 0.81 before adversarial training, 0.81 after.
  • transformer on real human-written injections: AUC 0.57 before adversarial training, 0.64 after.
  • transformer windowed on real human-written injections: AUC 0.72 before adversarial training, 0.76 after.

2 · Arms race: an evolving attacker vs a retraining defender

Each round the attacker mutates payloads for 12 generations against the current defender; the defender then retrains on what got through. Every round uses fresh malicious goals, so later rounds test whether patching generalised. Attacker success = share of the final population that evades detection and is still understood by the victim oracle.

Limitations (read these)

  • Attacks and benign prompts are synthetic, built from templates. Malicious goals are split by string between train/test/arena, but they share a vocabulary. High in-distribution numbers say little; the out-of-family and real-data numbers are the honest ones.
  • The victim oracle is a keyword-recovery proxy, not a real LLM. It can accept mutants a real model would not follow (e.g. heavy interleaving) and reject some it would.
  • Thresholds are calibrated on synthetic benign text. On real data the operating point does not transfer, which is why AUC is reported alongside recall/FPR.
  • Three defenders are linear models over TF-IDF; two are one small fine-tuned transformer (MiniLM-L6, 160 optimiser steps per fit, CPU budget). A larger model or a longer schedule may behave differently, so read the transformer rows as "a small fine-tuned encoder does not close the gap", not as a statement about all transformers.
